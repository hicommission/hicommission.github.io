VideoOrganizer – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10-22-2026

VideoOrganizer (“the App”) is designed to help you organize, browse, and manage your YouTube playlists more efficiently. We take your privacy seriously. This Privacy Policy explains what information we collect, how it is used, and the choices you have.

1. Information We Collect

1.1 Google Account Information (With Your Permission)

If you choose to sign in with your Google account, the App receives:

  • Your Google account identifier (non-sensitive)
  • YouTube playlist metadata (playlist name, ID, item count, thumbnails)
  • Public video metadata needed to display your playlists

We do NOT access, collect, or store:

  • Your Google password
  • Your private videos
  • Your comments, messages, or contact lists
  • Any sensitive personal data

1.2 App Usage Data

We run no analytics, tracking, or logging services of our own, and the App contains no advertising or analytics SDKs. On Android only, the camera text scan uses Google ML Kit, which sends usage and performance information, including a per-installation identifier (not your name or account), to Google (see §10).

2. How We Use Your Information

We use the data we access only to:

  • Display your YouTube playlists inside the App
  • Allow you to browse, sort, and filter your playlists
  • Improve app stability and user experience

We do not use your information for:

  • Advertising
  • Tracking
  • Profiling
  • Selling data to third parties

3. Data Storage & Security

  • No personal data is stored on our servers.
  • Playlist and account data retrieved from Google APIs is kept only on your device.
  • All communication with Google services uses secure, encrypted connections (HTTPS).

4. Third-Party Services

VideoOrganizer uses:

  • Google Sign-In to authenticate your account
  • YouTube Data API to access your playlists

Your use of these services is subject to Google’s Privacy Policy and Terms of Service.

Data goes only to the Google services described in this policy, to perform the features you use, and (for the Android video player) to GitHub Pages as our hosting provider. We do not sell your data or share it with anyone else, and we operate no servers that receive your data.

5. Your Choices

5.1 Disconnect Google Account

You may sign out at any time. When you sign out:

  • Your sign-in is removed from the device and the App no longer accesses your Google account
  • On iOS, all playlist data is removed from your device
  • On Android, tags, favorites, See Also links, imported history and the PhotoFinder index stay on the device until you clear the App’s storage or uninstall it (see §10 and §11)

5.2 Delete the App

Deleting the App permanently removes all locally stored data.

6. Children’s Privacy

VideoOrganizer is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

7. Changes to This Privacy Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy occasionally. The updated version will be posted in the App and/or on our website.

9. YouTube API Services

VideoOrganizer uses YouTube API Services to show and manage your YouTube playlists. By using VideoOrganizer you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service. Google’s handling of your data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

You can remove VideoOrganizer’s access to your Google account at any time in your Google Account security settings: https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions.

10. Android App — Additional Details

The Android version of VideoOrganizer includes a few features that work differently from the iOS version:

  • Sign-in: Google sign-in opens in your web browser (Chrome when it is installed). The App asks only for access to your YouTube account.
  • PhotoFinder (optional): if you open PhotoFinder and allow photo access, the App reads the date and location saved in the photos on your device to build a searchable index on your device. To show place names it uses Android’s built-in geocoder, which may send photo coordinates to Google’s location services. Your photos themselves are not uploaded.
  • Camera text scan: text recognition runs on your device using Google ML Kit. ML Kit sends usage and performance information, including a per-installation identifier (not your name or account), to Google.
  • Search: what you search for is sent to YouTube to return results. That includes text you type, text scanned with the camera, text pasted from or detected on the clipboard, and a video title when you open See Also > YouTube. The App reads the clipboard when it opens or returns to the foreground, to detect YouTube links; clipboard text is sent only when it is used as a search or to add a video.
  • Playlist changes: playlists you create or rename (title and description), and videos you add, remove or reorder, are sent to YouTube to update your account.
  • Stored on your device: tags, favorites, See Also links, imported watch history and the Recently Deleted list are stored only on your device. They stay on the device after you sign out and are removed when you uninstall the App.
  • Video player: videos play in the official YouTube embedded player. YouTube receives the video you play and your interactions with the player, and may store cookies in the App’s player. The player page is hosted on GitHub Pages (hicommission.github.io), which receives the video ID and your IP address as our hosting provider.

Data goes only to the Google services described above, to perform the features you use, and to GitHub Pages as our hosting provider. We do not sell your data or share it with anyone else, and we operate no servers that receive your data.

11. Deleting Your Data

  • We keep no copy of your data, so there is nothing for us to delete on a server.
  • To remove everything the App stores on your device: sign out, then go to Settings > Apps > VideoOrganizer > Storage > Clear storage, or uninstall the App.
  • To remove the App’s access to your Google account: https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions.
  • Playlist changes you made live in your YouTube account and are managed in YouTube.
